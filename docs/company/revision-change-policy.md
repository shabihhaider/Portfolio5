# Revision & Change Request Policy — Shabih.

---

## The Difference: Revision vs. Change Request

This is the most important distinction to understand — and to communicate to clients upfront.

| | Revision | Change Request |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Adjusting what was agreed | Adding something new |
| **Examples** | Change button color, update text, fix alignment | Add a new page, integrate a new API, add a feature |
| **Cost** | Included (2 rounds) | Quoted separately |
| **Requires new SOW?** | No | Yes |

**Rule:** If the client is asking for something that was in the original SOW, it's a revision. If they're asking for something that was not in the original SOW, it's a Change Request.

## Revision Policy

### What's Included
- **2 rounds of revisions** per project, included in every fixed-price quote
- Each round covers one consolidated list of changes submitted by the client

### What Counts as One Round
A revision round starts when the client submits their feedback and ends when those changes are implemented and re-delivered. The client cannot add to a round after the Developer begins implementing the feedback.

**One round = one consolidated message or document with all feedback for that review cycle.**

### How to Handle Piecemeal Feedback
Clients sometimes submit feedback item by item ("Also can you change...", "Actually one more thing..."). When this happens:
1. Do not implement feedback until the client confirms they are done
2. Respond: *"I'll note all of this. Just let me know when you've gone through everything and I'll incorporate it all at once."*
3. Once they confirm, that becomes Round 1

### Turnaround Time
- Revision rounds are typically turned around in 48–72 hours
- Communicate a specific date when starting each revision round

### Round Tracking
Keep track of which round you're on in every project. Note it in your message: *"Here's the updated version after Round 1. You have one more revision round included."*

### What Happens After 2 Rounds
After 2 rounds are used, any further revisions are charged at **$50/hr** (minimum 1 hour). Communicate this clearly before the client's second revision round begins: *"This is your second and final included revision round. After this, any additional changes will be billed at $50/hr."*

## Change Request Policy

### When a Change Request Applies
Any time a client asks for something outside the original SOW:
- Adding a new page or section
- Adding a feature not in the original spec
- Integrating a new third-party service
- Redesigning a section that was already built and approved
- Any work that adds time to the project

### How to Handle Change Requests

**Step 1: Identify it as a change request**
Respond: *"That sounds like a great addition. Since it's outside the original scope, I'll put together a quick quote for this as a separate add-on."*

**Step 2: Quote it**
Estimate the additional time and provide a fixed price (or hourly estimate if complex).

**Step 3: Get written approval**
Client must confirm the additional scope and price in writing (Upwork message, email) before work begins.

**Step 4: Add it to the project**
Only begin the additional work after written approval is received.

### Change Request Form Template

When quoting a change request, use this format:

```
CHANGE REQUEST — [Project Name]
Date: [Date]

Additional Work Requested:
[Description of what the client wants]

Additional Cost: $[Amount]
Estimated Additional Time: [X days]

This work is outside the original scope of work dated [Date].
Please confirm to proceed.
```

## Common Scenarios & How to Handle Them

**"Can you just quickly change the homepage design after we approved it?"**
→ If the design was already approved and built, this is a revision (uses one revision round).

**"Can you add a blog to the landing page we agreed on?"**
→ This is a Change Request. Quote it separately.

**"The contact form is broken."**
→ Bug in delivered work within 30 days of delivery = fixed for free, not a revision.

**"Can you update the copy on the About page?"**
→ Depends. If they're providing the new copy, it's a revision (text swap). If they want you to write it, that's out of scope.

**"We want to add Stripe payments."**
→ Change Request. Quote separately.

## Post-Delivery Bug Policy

Bugs in the delivered work (things that should work but don't, per the SOW) are fixed for **free within 30 days of delivery**. This is not a revision round — it's warranty work.

A bug is:
- A broken form
- A link that doesn't work
- A layout that breaks on a specific browser that was in scope
- Code that doesn't do what was agreed in the SOW

A bug is **not**:
- Something the client forgot to ask for
- A design preference change
- Third-party service outages (Stripe, Vercel, etc.)
