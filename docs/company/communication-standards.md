# Communication Standards — Shabih.

> Internal guide for how to communicate with clients professionally. Read this before starting a new project.

---

## Response Time

- **Standard:** Respond to all client messages within **24 hours** on any active project day
- **During active builds:** Send a progress update at minimum every **2 days**, even if there's nothing blocking — clients get anxious when they don't hear anything
- **Weekends:** Best effort. No hard SLA, but don't go 3+ days silent during an active project

If you're going to miss the 24-hour window for any reason (illness, emergency, heavy workload), message the client proactively: *"Just flagging that I'll be slower to respond today, but I'll be back on track tomorrow."*

---

## Official Communication Channels

| Channel | Use For |
|---|---|
| **Email** | Sending agreements, invoices, formal decisions |
| **Upwork Messages** | All communication for Upwork clients (creates a paper trail) |
| **WhatsApp** | Quick updates during active projects (if client prefers) |
| **Slack** | If client sets up a shared workspace |

**Rule:** Any agreement, scope change, or payment confirmation must be in writing — email or Upwork messages only. WhatsApp is for casual updates, never for binding decisions.

---

## Tone & Professionalism

- Be friendly, direct, and clear — not corporate and stiff, but not casual either
- Use first-person ("I'm working on X" not "We are currently in progress of X")
- Keep messages short — clients don't want to read essays
- Never use jargon the client won't understand without explaining it
- If you make a mistake, own it immediately and state what you're doing to fix it

---

## Progress Updates (Template)

Send these every 1–2 days during an active build.

**Format:**
```
Quick update on [Project Name]:

✅ Done: [What was completed]
🔨 In progress: [What I'm working on now]
⏭ Next: [What's coming next]

[Optional: Any questions or blockers that need client input]

On track for [delivery date]. Let me know if you have any questions!
```

## Handling Scope Creep (Script)

When a client asks for something outside the SOW:

> *"That's a great idea. Since it's outside what we originally agreed on, I'd treat it as a separate add-on. I can put together a quick quote for this — want me to do that? Alternatively, we can keep the current project on track and revisit this as a follow-up project after delivery."*

Never say "sure, I can add that" without checking whether it's in scope first.

---

## Handling Unhappy Clients (Script)

When a client is frustrated with progress or a deliverable:

> *"I understand your concern, and I appreciate you telling me directly. Let me review what you've described and get back to you with a specific plan within [X hours]. I want to make sure this is right."*

- Do not get defensive
- Do not promise things you can't deliver
- Do not go silent — respond even if you don't have a solution yet

---

## Handling Non-Responsive Clients

When a client stops responding:

- **Day 3 of no response:** Gentle follow-up: *"Just checking in on [Project Name] — still waiting on [X] from you. Let me know if you have any questions."*
- **Day 7 of no response:** *"I wanted to follow up again on [Project Name]. I've paused work while waiting for [X]. If I don't hear back by [Date], I'll assume the project is on hold and may need to reschedule it."*
- **Day 14 of no response:** *"I haven't been able to reach you for 2 weeks. I'm putting the project on hold. Please reach out when you're ready to continue — a re-activation fee may apply."*
- **Day 30 of no response:** Project is officially abandoned. Deposit is retained. Send final notice email.

---

## Handling Payment Issues

When a client delays the final payment:

- **Day 1 after delivery:** Send invoice with the final delivery
- **Day 7:** *"Just a reminder that the final invoice for [Project Name] is outstanding. Please confirm when payment will be processed."*
- **Day 14:** *"The final invoice for [Project Name] is now 14 days overdue. I'll need to receive payment before I can transfer credentials or source code."*
- **Day 21:** Consult the cancellation/refund policy. Do not hand over credentials until paid.

**Rule:** Never hand over login credentials, source code, or API keys before the final payment clears.

---

## What Not to Do

- Do not over-promise delivery dates to win a project
- Do not start work before the deposit is received
- Do not agree to scope changes verbally without writing them down
- Do not disappear during an active project without communicating first
- Do not respond to angry messages immediately — wait 15–30 minutes, then respond calmly
- Do not discuss pricing or rates for future work informally — always put it in writing
