# Upwork Rules & Best Practices — Shabih.

> Critical rules for operating on Upwork. Read before sending any proposal.

---

## Non-Negotiable Rules

### 1. Never Move Payments Off-Platform (First Contract)
Never accept or request payment outside Upwork for the first contract with any client. Upwork's payment protection is the entire reason the platform fee is worth paying. Moving off-platform on the first contract violates Upwork's Terms of Service and removes all dispute protection.

After a client relationship is established (2+ completed contracts, strong trust), direct payments may be appropriate — but never on the first project.

### 2. Everything in Writing on Upwork
Even if you've discussed scope on a call, put the summary in writing in Upwork messages. The Upwork message thread is your paper trail. If a dispute ever goes to Upwork, the messages are the primary evidence.

### 3. Never Start Work Before the Contract is Active
A client saying "go ahead and start" is not a contract. Work only begins when:
- A contract is created on Upwork
- The milestone is funded (for fixed-price contracts)

### 4. Use Milestones for Fixed-Price Work
For any project over $200, break it into milestones:
- Milestone 1: 50% deposit — released to start work
- Milestone 2: 50% on delivery — released after client review

Never have a single unfunded milestone. Funded milestones are the only payment protection Upwork offers.

### 5. Hourly Contracts Need Time Tracker
If using an hourly contract, use the Upwork desktop app and time tracker. Manual time is not covered by Upwork's payment protection. Tracked time is automatically billed and protected.

## Profile & Positioning

- **Headline:** Keep it client-outcome focused, not skill-list focused
  - Bad: "React, Next.js, Node.js, WordPress Developer"
  - Good: "Landing Pages & AI Integrations | Fast Delivery, Clean Code"
- **Availability:** Keep it updated. "Available" means clients see you first in search.
- **Rate:** Set your hourly rate on Upwork at your full rate ($50/hr). You can negotiate for specific projects, but starting low is hard to reverse.
- **Profile overview:** Write it from the client's perspective — what problem do you solve? What do they get?

## Proposals

### Good Proposal Structure
1. **First line:** Address their specific problem (not "Hi, I'm Shabih and I'm a developer")
2. **Middle:** Show you've read their brief — reference something specific they mentioned
3. **Your solution:** Briefly explain what you'd do and why it works
4. **Social proof:** One specific result or project that's relevant
5. **CTA:** Ask a relevant question to start a conversation, or state your availability and invite them to discuss

### Length
- Keep proposals under 200 words for most projects
- Long proposals are rarely read in full
- Quality over length — a focused 100-word proposal beats a rambling 400-word one

### What NOT to Do in Proposals
- Do not copy-paste the same generic proposal to every job
- Do not lead with your skills list
- Do not mention your rate before establishing value
- Do not bid on jobs you cannot deliver

## Pricing on Upwork

Upwork's fee structure (as of May 2025) is a **variable 0–15% per contract**, set by Upwork at the time you submit a proposal. The rate is visible before you submit. In practice, most freelancers report 10–15%, averaging around 12–13%.

The old tiered model (20%/10%/5%) was eliminated in May 2023. Do not use those numbers.

**Practical rule:** Add **15% to any target amount** to arrive at your Upwork quote. This covers the worst case.

| You want to receive | Quote on Upwork |
|---|---|
| $400 | $460 |
| $800 | $920 |
| $1,500 | $1,725 |

Always check the fee shown on the proposal page before submitting — if Upwork shows a lower rate on that specific contract, adjust accordingly.

## Reviews & Ratings

- A 5-star Upwork profile is the single biggest growth lever in the first year
- After every successful project, send the testimonial request (see onboarding-checklist.md)
- If a client gives less than 5 stars, it's worth sending a polite message asking if there was anything you could have done better — but never argue or pressure for a higher rating
- Your public response to a review is visible to all future clients. Always respond professionally, even to critical reviews.

## Identifying Problem Clients (Red Flags)

Watch for these before accepting a project:
- Vague brief with no clear scope ("just build me a website")
- Unrealistic budget (wants $2,000 work for $50)
- History of bad reviews on other freelancers' profiles
- Pressures you to start before the contract is active
- Wants to communicate only off-platform
- Asks you to do a "test task" for free
- Changes scope during proposal conversation
- Says "previous freelancer did bad job" without taking any responsibility

If you see 2+ of these, decline politely.

## Handling Disputes on Upwork

If a client opens a dispute:
1. Respond promptly and professionally within 24 hours
2. Gather all evidence: messages, SOW, deliverables, milestone history
3. Stick to facts — do not be emotional or accusatory
4. Upwork's dispute team reviews the message history — this is why everything in writing matters
5. For fixed-price disputes, Upwork holds funds in escrow — neither party gets them until resolved

Prevention is better than cure: a clear SOW, milestone structure, and written communication makes disputes rare and easy to win when they happen.
